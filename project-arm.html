PROJECT 01

Low-Cost, AI-Integrated Pick-and-Place Robotic Arm

A desktop five-joint robotic arm that finds objects with a camera and picks them up by name, with no taught positions. An overhead camera and the YOLOE open-vocabulary detector locate each object; a calibrated camera-to-table mapping, inverse kinematics and smooth cubic trajectories then guide the arm. It was designed in SOLIDWORKS, 3D-printed in PLA and built from locally available parts for ₦252,531.

Motion study

The problem

Industrial pick-and-place robots are expensive and are programmed for fixed positions and fixed products. Small manufacturers whose products change often can't justify them.

What I built

  • A five-joint arm (base, shoulder, elbow, wrist pitch, wrist roll) with a two-finger gripper, driven by six servos.
  • Link lengths 159, 120, 50.69 and 96.5 mm, giving a reach of about 426 mm. Structural parts are FDM 3D-printed in PLA.
  • A laptop runs detection, camera-to-table calibration (homography), inverse kinematics and trajectory planning. An Arduino Uno with a PCA9685 driver only drives the servos, which keeps the controller simple and the software easy to test.
  • Open-vocabulary detection: a new object is added by typing its name, with no image collection or labelling. This replaced a fixed-class YOLOv8n model that missed several objects seen from above.
  • It runs as an autonomous loop: look, choose the most confident reachable object, pick it, lift it to the home pose, place it and look again, until the table is clear. All 11 automated software tests passed.
  • Test set-up: a wooden camera stand that I designed in SOLIDWORKS and built, holding the overhead webcam above a marked work table, with the laptop beside the arm.

Engineering analysis

  • Forward and inverse kinematics checked to within 10−12 mm.
  • Factor of safety 13.3 at the wrist link and 124 at the base.
  • Servo torque checked against datasheets: payload limit 176 g at 5 V, against a 100 g design payload.
  • Cubic joint trajectories, so every joint starts and stops at zero speed.

Accuracy is limited by the open-loop hobby servos, not by the AI, the structure or the maths. The detector found every object, the parts deflect less than 0.5 mm, and the about 6 mm scatter matches roughly 1° of servo dead band (1° at 250 mm moves the fingertip 4.4 mm). The next step would be servos with position feedback.
